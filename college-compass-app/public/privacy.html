Privacy policy

Tierwise · Last updated October 27, 2026

Tierwise helps high-school students build a balanced college list. It is designed so that we, the developer, never see your data.

About Tierwise and AI

Tierwise uses artificial intelligence and public data, including the U.S. Department of Education's College Scorecard, to help you research colleges. AI can make mistakes. Admission chances, costs, deadlines and program details are estimates that may be incomplete or out of date. Always verify important information with each college's official sources before you make decisions. Tierwise is an independent research aid: it does not make or predict admission decisions, and it is not affiliated with or endorsed by any college or university, the College Board, ACT, or the Common Application.

The app shows this notice before your first research run and keeps it under Settings → About Tierwise.

What stays on your device

Everything you enter and everything the app produces is stored only on your phone (or in your browser, for the web version):

  • Your answers to the questions (home state, grades, test scores, interests, budget, income band, preferences)
  • Researched college data, your list, shortlist and notes
  • Your chat history with the Ask feature
  • Your ChatGPT sign-in tokens

None of this leaves your device except as described below. There are no Tierwise accounts. You can export, import or delete all of it at any time from Settings.

Shared college database

Tierwise keeps a shared database of facts about colleges (for example admit rates, test score ranges, costs, net price by family income, deadlines, programs and the sources they came from). It is hosted on Supabase and contains no information about students.

  • Federal data. Most figures come from the U.S. Department of Education's College Scorecard. When you research colleges, the app looks up the colleges' names in this database. Only the college names are sent, with no information about you.
  • Shared research (Settings → "Share research to help other students", on by default). After researching a college, the app sends the researched college facts listed above to the shared database, so later students can skip that research. It sends nothing about you: no answers, grades, scores, interests, notes, name, email, ChatGPT account or device identifiers. To recognize that two results came from different installs, the app includes a random ID created on first use; the database stores it only as a one-way hash, and it is not linked to you, your account or your device. To prevent abuse, the database also counts requests per network address for up to two days, stored only as a one-way hash. On iPhone, the app also uses Apple's App Attest to prove that shared facts come from the genuine app: it creates a key that only this app on your device can use and signs each contribution with it. The key does not identify you or your device, and a reinstall creates a new one. Shared facts are published only after independent research from another install agrees. You can turn sharing off at any time in Settings.

Counselor listings

"Get expert help" in the app shows a directory of independent college counselors who pay to be listed. It works like this:

  • Browsing sends nothing about you. When you open the directory, the app sends only the filters you choose (a state and specialties) to the shared database, with the same publishable key used for college facts. No answers, grades, notes, name, account or device identifiers are sent, and counselors never learn who viewed their listing.
  • Students' data is never shared with counselors. There is no messaging in the app. If you choose to contact a counselor, you do it yourself through their website, phone or email, outside the app.
  • What we store about counselors. For each counselor: the listing they write (name, title, photo, bio, credentials, states, specialties, contact details), their account email, review notes and Stripe billing identifiers. Counselor listings are reviewed by us before they appear.
  • Reports. If you report a listing, the report (reason and any text you type) is stored without your identity. To prevent abuse, requests are counted per network address for a short time, stored only as a one-way hash.
  • Listings are advertising. They never influence which colleges the app recommends.

What is sent to OpenAI

Research and chat are powered by ChatGPT. When you run research or ask a question, the app sends your profile answers, the names of colleges being researched and your question directly from your device to OpenAI's API, under your own ChatGPT account and subject to OpenAI's terms and privacy policy. Requests are made with the "do not store" option. The developer of Tierwise does not receive, relay or see these requests.

Usage counts toward your ChatGPT plan. You can review it at chatgpt.com.

What we don't do

  • No analytics, tracking, advertising or crash-reporting SDKs
  • No third-party services other than OpenAI, which you connect yourself, and the shared database described above (college facts and counselor listings)
  • No sale or sharing of personal data — we never collect it

Children

The app is intended for high-school students, typically 14 and older, often together with a parent. Because nothing is collected by the developer, there is no account to delete; removing the app or choosing "Delete everything" in Settings erases all data.

Signing out

Signing out removes your ChatGPT tokens from the device. You can also revoke the app's access from your ChatGPT account settings.
